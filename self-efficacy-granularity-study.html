Mastery experience in the logs

Self-efficacy (15 items, Steinhorst et al. 2020, n = 93), measured in May–June 2025, at the end of TE23’s course and about eleven months after TE22’s, predicted from 118 log features grounded in social cognitive theory. It follows the approach of book ch. 7 (Saqr & López-Pernas 2024): compute the features for a window of the course, fit a random forest, then move the window. The comparison covers two granularities (teaching weeks, content units) and three window schemes (forward, backward, rolling).

  • The logs carry a real but modest signal. The best models explain 14–29% of the variance in end-of-course self-efficacy (cross-validated R² .14–.29, Spearman ρ .37–.54) and separate High from Low with AUC up to .79. Every model put through a permutation test beat all 100 label-shuffled runs (p = .01). The corrected t-test against the mean baseline is more conservative and gives p from < .001 to .03 per window, and with 80 model-versus-baseline tests two survive Holm correction (one for the score, one for High/Low), so read single windows as indicative.
  • Weeks and units perform about the same. The best week window (weeks 1–35, family factors, R² .291) edges out the best unit window (units 3–11, family factors, R² .208), but the difference is not significant (corrected t-test, two-sided p = .06; full course .126 vs .157 with raw features, p = .32). Units give steadier results for short windows, while weeks reach the signal earlier in calendar time.
  • Forward: little in the first month. Weeks 1–4 and units 1–3 stay low (R² ≤ .07, apart from a single .11 for weeks 1–2), and the 10×10 re-check of weeks 1–4 gives R² .04. The first window to reach R² .15 is weeks 1–10 (R² .18 in the re-check), roughly when the class finishes unit 4. For units it is units 1–9 (R² .17, around week 25). After that the curves level off, and later data adds little.
  • Backward: recent weeks alone say little, but dropping the first weeks helps. The last 1–8 weeks give R² ≤ .10. The final unit (final-project period, about 10 weeks) reaches ≈ .13. Windows that start around week 6/7 and run to the end give the best backward results, which suggests the shared early-course activity mostly adds noise.
  • Window length matters more than granularity. Rolling 4-week windows swing between about −.03 and .23 depending on position. 3-unit windows are steadier (.05–.14 after the first units), and the best rolling windows fit the two cohorts about equally (ρ .43 TE22, .39 TE23). Across all positions, mean R² rises from about .06–.07 for 1-week or 1-unit windows to about .13–.16 for 16-week or 5-unit windows, and at matched length week windows are ahead.
  • Family factors come close to raw features. Across all 249 windows the raw features are slightly ahead on average (random forest mean R² .123 vs .118, raw higher in 50% of windows). In the 10 re-checked key windows the difference is not significant in any of them. With 22 predictors instead of ~60 and clear SCT labels, the factors are a reasonable default for the grade models and the clustering. The random forest and elastic net never differ significantly after multiplicity correction.
  • What goes with high self-efficacy fits social cognitive theory. The strongest correlates include completing the exercises one attempts (ρ = .38, mastery experience), fewer repeated errors (error quotient ρ = −.37), recovering from an error on the next run (ρ = .37), and regular practice (median gap between active days ρ = −.38). All point the same way in both cohorts; with 31 TE23 students not all are significant there.

Self-efficacy was measured once, at the end. TE22 answered about 11 months after their course ended. These are associations with a later self-report, not effects, and "early" windows predict an end-of-course state rather than a future change.

How it was set up

The decisions that matter for reading the charts.

Data and target

2.25 million events from 153 students (without the students who declined the use of their CS Academy data, with survey answers used only where the student consented), each cohort's own course year (TE22 2023/24, TE23 2024/25, with TE23 cut at their survey time). Blur, resize and auto-reload events are excluded because they were logged in one year only. Self-efficacy is the mean of the answered items (at least 12 of 15): α = .96, ω = .96, and one dominant factor (eigenvalue 9.9, next 1.05). High/Low is a median split at 5.27, as in book ch. 7.

Weeks and units

Teaching weeks count from each cohort's first Monday, with holiday weeks merged (TE22 37 weeks, TE23 36). Units are content-based: every event is assigned to the CS1 unit of the exercise or notes page it belongs to, whenever it happened. Unit 11 is units 11–12, including the final project. Notes pages get the unit of the next exercise opened.

Features and factors

All 118 features in the dictionary, recomputed from events for each of the 249 windows. Sessions break after 30 minutes without events, and active time counts gaps of up to 5 minutes. Sub-second duplicate runs are collapsed. The factor analysis is run within each of the 8 families: parallel analysis, minres with varimax, fitted on all 153 logged students without the outcome. It gives 22 factors for the full course.

Models and validation

Random forest (default mtry), an elastic net or L2-logistic baseline, and a mean baseline. Each is fitted on raw features (with book ch. 7's |r| > .75 filter) and on family factors. Validation is repeated stratified 10-fold CV: 3 repeats for all 249 windows, and 10 repeats on fresh splits with 500 trees for the 10 key windows, plus corrected t-tests, permutation tests and cohort transfer.

Model Metric

Forward: growing windows from the course start

Each point is a model trained on everything from the start up to that point. Unit windows sit at the teaching week by which the class had done 75% of its work on the last unit included. Shaded bands show ±1 SD across CV repeats. The grey line is the mean-prediction baseline. The values behind all window charts, for every model and metric, are in one file: self-efficacy-windows.csv (2,490 windows × models).

Story thread · When it shows

In their first week, three in four students are in the most active weekly state, and who ends the year in the top half for exercises passed can already be predicted from two weeks of activity (AUC .82; Chapter 3, prediction). Self-efficacy cannot. Early activity is high and alike; the differences that go with the belief build up later, as students finish, stall or fall behind (Chapter 4).

Which window length works best?

Mean random-forest R² across all positions of a rolling window, by window length. Week windows use every other start week. Unit lengths are converted to weeks at the course average of ≈3.3 weeks per unit, so the two granularities can be compared at matched length.

Family factors

Longer windows are better for both granularities, and no short window matches the cumulative ones. At matched length, week windows are ahead (3 units ≈ 10 weeks: R² .113 vs ≈ .152 for weeks with raw features, .100 vs ≈ .142 with factors). Even so, granularity is a secondary choice; how much of the course a window covers is the primary one.

Permutation tests

Labels shuffled 100 times and the whole CV pipeline re-run each time. Observed R² comes from one 10-fold split, which is why it differs slightly from the table above.

Does it transfer across cohorts?

Trained on one cohort only and tested on the other. The rank correlation in the test cohort is the fairest measure here, because R² also penalises the cohorts' different means and spreads. Each cell is Spearman ρ in the held-out cohort (→ TE23 means trained on TE22). Green marks ρ ≥ .30. Cumulative windows transfer (ρ ≈ .19–.43 for the random forest), and so do the two rolling windows (.23–.44).

Robustness (full course). Refitting the factor analysis inside each training fold, instead of once on all logged students, changes random forest R² from .201 to .193 and elastic net from .159 to .150. The pooled fit was at most slightly optimistic. Tuning mtry by out-of-bag error, as book ch. 7 does, gives .166 vs .156 with the default (raw features).

Story thread · Improves

This speaks to a puzzle in the Log atlas, where the pass rate of graded submissions was unrelated to self-efficacy (ρ .03) and controlling for exercise points was proposed as the test (Chapter 3). The completion rate of attempted exercises is close to that test, and it is among the strongest correlates here (ρ .38): finishing counts, however many tries it takes. Recovering from an error on the next run is the same idea at the scale of seconds; Chapter 6 shows which errors students learn to recover from faster.

Factor analysis by family

Full-course solution. Stability φ is Tucker's congruence between each window's factors and the full-course factors (median over forward and backward windows of 8 weeks / 3 units or longer). Values ≥ .85 count as similar, ≥ .95 as equal. KMO below .6 (trajectory, work strategy) means those two families barely factor.

Caveats

  • With n = 93 every estimate is wide. The window curves compare many windows, so their peaks are optimistic. Fresh splits reduce that for the key windows but don't remove it, because the students are the same.
  • TE22 answered the survey a year after their course. Pooling assumes the log–self-efficacy relation is similar across cohorts; the cohort-transfer check supports that for cumulative and rolling windows.
  • The 15-item scale leaves out the 5 OOP and recursion items of the published 20-item instrument. It is reliable here (α = .96) but not validated in this form.
  • Unit windows depend on content attribution. Notes pages take the unit of the next exercise opened, a heuristic, and 15% of events (sandbox, navigation) belong to no unit.
  • Verbal persuasion and vicarious experience, two of Bandura's four sources, leave no trace in these logs. The features cover mastery, persistence and self-regulation.

What this means

  • When to look. For early warning, cumulative week windows from about week 10 (or units 1–9) are the earliest that carry the signal. For explanation, the window from about week 6/7 to the end, or the full course, carries the most.
  • What to look at. Finishing the exercises a student attempts, getting past errors and practising regularly go with higher self-efficacy, and so does how often a student comes back (number of sessions ρ = .40).
  • How to use the factors. The 22 full-course family factors are nearly as good as the raw features and are named in social cognitive theory's terms. Chapter 5 clusters students on them.
