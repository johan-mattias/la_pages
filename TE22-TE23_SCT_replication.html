Reading the numbers

  • ρ is a Spearman correlation on percentile ranks within each class, pooled over the classes, so differences in level between classes and teachers do not drive it. Brackets and lines are 95% bootstrap intervals; an interval that excludes zero counts as holding.
  • Self-efficacy and the other survey items are means on the seven-point answer scale. g is Hedges' g; p values come from Welch tests, with Holm's correction where it says so.
  • Grades are coded A = 5 to F = 0.
  • Self-efficacy was measured once: a year after the course for TE22 and at its end for TE23. Its links with grades are associations, not effects on later grades.

  • Five classes: te22a, te22b and te22c (TE22, programming course in 2023/24), te23b and te23c (TE23, course in 2024/25). Teacher 1 taught te22b and te22c; teacher 2 taught te22a and both TE23 classes.

  • The WiPSCE Round 1 data from these classes held — answers without a yes to consent: a no in te22c and in te23c, and a blank in te22a. They are left out here; the draft's Round 1 figures would move slightly without them.
  • One te22a survey code matches no student. The answer counts in the survey analyses but cannot be linked to grades or logs.
  • One te23b grade row has no user id and is assumed to belong to the one te23b student without a grade row.
  • te23c's final grade is its final-project grade. Its "final" column is the grade of 9 May 2025, set before the final projects were handed in, and is kept only for reference (as in Chapter 10).
  • — near-blank Year 1 tests, with more than half the items unanswered, are left out.
  • The names in the platform's progress files were never read, and the page shows no single students.

Methods

  • Self-efficacy: parallel analysis and principal axis factoring with oblimin rotation; omega from a Schmid–Leiman solution; confirmatory models with robust maximum likelihood. Scores are item means, given when at least half the items are answered.
  • Correlations: Spearman on percentile ranks within each class, pooled, with bootstrap intervals.
  • Year 1 test: the answer key reproduces every recorded score. Block and text versions of the same task are compared with McNemar's test and Holm's correction.
  • Logs: an active minute has at least one event. Lessons are found from times when a large part of the class was active together; activity outside them counts as outside lessons.
  • Final-grade weights: LMG relative importance among students with every grade in their group.

Caveats

  • Self-efficacy was measured after the grades: a year after the course for TE22, at its end for TE23. Correlations with grades can run either way.
  • The feedback date is an estimate from hand-ins, not a logged event, and logged minutes miss work done off the platform, such as the final projects made locally.
  • Response rates differ by class, and in the earlier report respondents had higher grades than non-respondents.
  • With five classes, class composition, teacher and year cannot be separated.
  • Only te22 took the follow-up test, — students.
