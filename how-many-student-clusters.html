How many student clusters?

One. In both time windows, the methods from chapters 8 and 9 of Learning Analytics Methods and Applications agree that the students form a single continuous cloud. Model-based clustering adds a second, smaller profile, but it is a spread-out tail of less regular, more error-prone students around the same centre. It is not a separate group.

Chapter 8 · k-means, PAM and hierarchical clustering

No value of k separates the students

The silhouette width measures how much closer each student is to their own cluster than to the nearest other one. Across k = 2 to 8 and all three algorithms it never gets past . For k = 2 to 4 it sits inside the range the same data gives when it is simulated as one population. The gap statistic picks K = 1, and the elbow curve bends smoothly with no kink.

Chapter 9 · Gaussian mixture models with mclust

mclust prefers two components

Chapter 9 fits Gaussian mixtures with 1 to 9 components under 14 covariance structures and compares them by BIC and ICL, where higher is better. Both criteria pick VEE with two components in both windows. Entropy () and the average posterior probabilities (at least ) clear the chapter's thresholds of 0.6 and 0.8.

Extra checks

Why two components are not two groups

A mixture of two normal curves can describe one skewed, heavy-tailed cloud just as well as two separate groups. These checks tell the two apart.

    Solution

    Across windows the tail is partly the same students. Of the 22 full-course tail students still active in weeks 8/9 to end, 16 are in that window's tail. Agreement across all students is low (ARI 0.15) because the later tail is larger.

    Next steps

    For the paper

    • Report no evidence of distinct subgroups. Silhouette widths are low at every k and no better than a one-population simulation, the gap statistic picks one cluster, and the fitted mixture has one peak.
    • If a person-centred result is still wanted, use the two-profile mclust solution. Report its entropy and AvePP, and describe it as a typical majority plus a smaller at-risk tail with sparse, error-prone activity, not as two student types.
    • Treat the factors as continuous dimensions in the self-efficacy analyses. If clusters are compared on self-efficacy, justify it through social cognitive theory rather than the prediction results, since these factors were chosen partly because they predict it.
    • To test the tail reading directly, fit heavy-tailed or skewed mixtures such as multivariate t or skew-normal (the R packages teigen or mixsmsn). Those have not been run yet.
    Story thread · Stalls and drops

    Other chapters group students too. The four working styles in Chapter 3 separate on outcomes but are not discrete types, the start-and-finish profiles in Chapter 1 are descriptive, and the activity trajectories in Chapter 4 recur in shape but not in membership. This chapter asks the question directly and finds one continuous population, so read every profile on the site as a region of that continuum. Its tail, sparse activity with error-ended sessions and high error proneness, is where the story's drops sit.

    Data. 159 students in two cohorts. Clustering uses the factors marked for clustering in the factor key: sessions, progress and mastery, active-day regularity, and debugging error proneness. That is 10 factors for the full course and 11 for weeks 8/9 to end. All factors were z-scored, and the two students inactive in the later window were dropped. No self-efficacy comparisons are shown here.

    Methods. R 4.3.3 with mclust 6.0.1 and cluster 2.1.6, following chapter 8 and chapter 9. The one-population simulations use a Gaussian copula with each factor's exact empirical distribution.

    Files. In the research repository, findings/clustering_findings.md and findings/clustering_report_2026-09-29.md explain the work, analyses/clustering/ holds the R and Python scripts (run in order by run_all.sh), and data/derived/clustering/ holds every table, log and plot.
