TE22 vs TE23 in Programmering 1

The same learning-analytics measures for the two full-year cohorts: TE22 (te22a, te22b, te22c; 2023/24) and TE23 (te23b, te23c; 2024/25). Each finding is checked in both cohorts; the ones that hold in both are the robust ones. Some are new, found in the full-year data. Two teachers taught these classes, both new to the platform in 2023/24, so the comparison also separates teacher and year where the classes allow it.

01The classes

Medians per student unless marked. TE22 = the three 2023/24 classes pooled, TE23 = the two 2024/25 classes pooled.

  • Final grade. TE22: the course grade in each class's grade file (also unit 4, 6, 7 or 10 tasks and the final project). TE23: the "final" column of the corrected grade files (also the unit-10 task, the final project and an estimated final grade).
  • Students. Everyone with platform activity is included; students without a grade are left out of grade comparisons only (te22b 2, te22c 1, te23c 1). Two te22b students have no events in the log, so log-based measures cover 32 of te22b's 34.
  • Exercises. Exercise counts and points cover the exercise sets (including challenge exercises), not the checkpoints inside the lesson pages; checkpoints are counted on their own in the persistence findings.

02How TE22 and TE23 differ

Per-student medians, with each class and both cohorts pooled. The p-value compares TE22 with TE23 (Mann–Whitney). TE22's time away is an estimate from return events and is not comparable in level, so it is left out. The next section shows which of these differences follow the teacher and which follow the year.

03Teacher and year

Teacher 1 taught te22b and te22c. Teacher 2 taught te22a in the same year, and te23b and te23c the year after. So te22a against teacher 1's classes shows a difference between teachers in their first year, and te22a against TE23 shows the same teacher a year later, with new students and a year more experience. Where te22a looks like teacher 1's classes and TE23 does not, the difference follows the year; where te22a already looks like TE23, it follows the teacher.

    05What predicts the grade

    Spearman correlations with the final grade and 95% bootstrap intervals. Each cohort pools its classes using ranks within each class (TE22 n about 90, TE23 about 67). Error and lesson-question measures use units 1–4. The two cohorts agree on almost every measure. The clear differences: very fast answers on lesson questions and time away go with lower grades only in TE23, and so does working outside lessons with higher ones.

    06Difficulty and time

    Share of each student's attempts at 2–3-point exercises. The full-year classes worked through whole exercise sets, so most students made a third or more of their attempts at the harder exercises. Within each class, taking on more of them goes with a higher grade.

    The time cost of difficulty is similar in every class. TE22 and te23c also took longer on the loop units (7–11) than on earlier units with the same points; te23b did not.

    07Pace

    In both cohorts the students who are usually behind the class median have the lowest grades, and the lead over the class median goes with the grade about as strongly (TE22 ρ 0.65, TE23 0.66). How many students end up ahead or behind depends on the teacher: teacher 1's classes moved in step, teacher 2's spread out in both years (see Teacher and year).

    08Errors

    Change in fix time per doubling of encounters with the same error type, for the same student, in units 1–4. Below zero = faster with practice. The specific syntax messages speed up in both cohorts (missing colon, indentation, brackets), faster in TE23. 'Invalid syntax' and NameError do not; in TE22 they even slow down.

    09Lesson pages

    Reading time before each multiple-choice answer, relative to other students on the same question, split into fifths. The fastest fifth answers worst in every class. TE23 answered less accurately overall (a median of 52% of first answers right vs 64%) and gave about twice as many very fast answers. te22a, teacher 2's first class, was closer to the other TE22 classes than to TE23, so this follows the year more than the teacher.

    10Creative code

    Programs from the submission zips, analysed without running them. The number of decisions (if, elif, loops and boolean conditions) a student builds into creative programs is one of the strongest grade links in both cohorts: for the final project, the graded unit task, the semester project and all creative programs together. Teacher 1's classes built larger semester and final projects; teacher 2's were about the same size in both years.

    11Self-efficacy

    Self-efficacy is the mean of the 15 SCT items (1–7). In TE22, answered a year after the course, it tracks the course grade. With the corrected grades it does so in te23b too (ρ 0.64), but not in te23c (0.09), so the TE23 pooled value is uncertain. In both cohorts it goes with practice volume and lesson-question accuracy.

    12Groups

    Every student was placed in the nearest of six behaviour profiles on 11 measures, with grades held out. The same grade ladder appears in both cohorts, and the measures relate to each other in much the same way (similarity of the correlation matrices, TE22 vs TE23: 0.73).

    13The video-game check

    In te24c (2025/26) four students were seen playing games in class. te24c was not a full course and is not part of the comparison, but it is the only class with observations, so the check starts there. Their logs show three things: much time away from the platform during lessons, many absences of a minute or more, and leaving in the same minutes as each other. The first two make a signature that can be computed for any class; the third tests whether high scorers form a group that leaves together. TE23's log has the same leave and return events as te24c's. TE22's has only return events, so its signature is an estimate.

      15Method notes

      • Same code, same rules. The scripts from the te24c analysis were run on each class, with only inputs, paths and course dates changed: profile cut-offs, persistence outcomes, error spells and slopes, reading-time measures, lesson detection, the 11 group measures and the bootstrap settings.
      • Pooling the cohorts. TE22 pools three classes and TE23 two. Correlations use ranks within each class, so differences in level between classes do not count as an effect. Error slopes and lesson-page models pool students with student fixed effects. Cohort differences in level compare per-student values (Mann–Whitney); differences between correlations use Fisher's z.
      • Teacher and year. With one teacher-2 class in 2023/24 and two in 2024/25, teacher and year are separated only through te22a, and class composition cannot be separated from either.
      • Submitted vs started. An unsolved exercise counts as submitted when the log has an autograde event for it. In te22a's export a score of 0 often appears without any submission (331 of 428 exercise and checkpoint cells), so a 0 in an export means the exercise was started.
      • Lesson pages. The platform locks a student out for at least 10 s after every second wrong answer, the same in both years.
      • Timestamps. TE23's export times are one hour ahead of Swedish time and were shifted back; TE22's match the log.
      • Privacy. The TE22 code-zip file names include real names after the username. Only the username is kept.
      • Video-game signature. Away = blur → focus gaps of at least 2 s during detected lessons (TE22: previous event → focus). Co-absence = correlation between two students' minute-by-minute away indicators after removing the class's away rate in that minute. Links = pairs above the class's 90th percentile.
      Scripts and the findings summary are saved in the project under claude/te23/ and claude/te22_te23_findings.md.
