TE22 vs TE23 in Programmering 1

The te24c analysis rerun on the two full-year cohorts: te22a, te22b and te22c from 2023/24, and te23b and te23c from 2024/25. Each te24c finding is checked in both cohorts, and the cohorts are compared directly. te24c was not a full course, so it is shown only as a reference. It is also the class where students were seen playing games, which makes it the test case for the video-game check.

te24c is the referenceIt was not a full course (one term, and its log covers units 1–4), so it is shown in grey and is not part of the comparison. The findings being checked come from it, and so does the video-game observation.
No blur events in 2023/24The TE22 log records when a student returns to the tab, not when they leave. Time away for TE22 is estimated from those return events. The estimate ranks students well (ρ 0.76–0.85 with the real measure), but its level is not comparable with the other years.
Self-efficacy at different timesTE22 answered in May 2025, a year after its course. TE23 answered in June 2025, at the end of its course (32 of 68). te24c answered before and after. In every class mostly the stronger students answered.
Same course, different yearsBoth cohorts took the full course over a school year, with the same 583 exercises and pages. TE22 had more lesson time on the platform and a longer final-project period.

01The classes

Medians per student unless marked. TE22 = the three 2023/24 classes pooled, TE23 = the two 2024/25 classes pooled. te24c (grey) is the reference.

  • Final grade. TE22: the course grade in each class's grade file (also unit 4, 6, 7 or 10 tasks and the final project). TE23: the course grade of 10 June 2025. te24c: the final grade.
  • Students. Everyone with platform activity is included; students without a grade are left out of grade comparisons only (te22b 2, te22c 1, te23c 1, te24c 1). Two te22b students have no events in the log, so log-based measures cover 32 of te22b's 34.
  • Exercises. Exercise counts and points cover the exercise sets (including challenge exercises), not the checkpoints inside the lesson pages; checkpoints are counted on their own in the persistence findings. The same rule is used in every class.

04What predicts the grade

Spearman correlations with the final grade and 95% bootstrap intervals. Each cohort pools its classes using ranks within each class (TE22 n about 90, TE23 about 67). Log measures use units 1–4, as in te24c. The two cohorts agree on almost every measure. Two differ: very fast answers on lesson questions go with lower grades only in TE23 (difference p = .003), and leaving exercises unsolved is tied more strongly to the grade in TE23 (p = .035).

05Difficulty and time

Share of each student's attempts at 2–3-point exercises. te24c's cut-offs (dashed) do not travel: the full-year classes worked through whole exercise sets, so most students sit above them. Within each class, taking on more of the harder exercises still goes with a higher grade.

The time cost of difficulty is similar everywhere. TE22 and te23c also took longer on the loop units (7–11) than on earlier units with the same points; te23b and te24c did not.

06Pace

In both cohorts the students who are usually behind the class median have the lowest grades, and the link between lead and grade is stronger than in te24c (TE22 ρ 0.65, TE23 0.61). TE22 moved more in step: in te22b and te22c most students stayed around the median all year.

07Errors

Change in fix time per doubling of encounters with the same error type, for the same student, in units 1–4. Below zero = faster with practice. The specific syntax messages speed up in both cohorts (missing colon, indentation, brackets), and faster in TE23. 'Invalid syntax' and NameError do not; in TE22 they even slow down.

08Lesson pages

Reading time before each multiple-choice answer, relative to other students on the same question, split into fifths. The fastest fifth answers worst in every class. TE23 answered less accurately overall (a median of 52% of first answers right vs 64%) and gave about twice as many very fast answers as TE22.

09Creative code

Programs from the submission zips, analysed without running them. Final projects were larger in TE22, especially te22b and te22c, and so were their semester projects; TE23's final projects match te24c's (median 121 vs 120 lines). In every class more decisions in the final project goes with a higher final grade.

10Self-efficacy

Self-efficacy is the mean of the 15 SCT items (1–7). In TE22, answered a year after the course, it tracks the course grade about as strongly as te24c's pre-course survey did. In TE23, answered at the end of the course, it barely does (32 answers), although it goes with practice volume and pace.

11Groups

Every student was placed in the te24c group whose profile on the 11 behaviour measures is closest, with grades held out. The same grade ladder appears in both cohorts. The measures relate to each other in much the same way (similarity of the correlation matrices: TE22 vs te24c 0.86, TE23 vs te24c 0.69, TE22 vs TE23 0.73).

12The video-game check

In te24c four students were seen playing games in class. te24c was not a full course, but it is the only class with observations, so the check starts there. Their logs show three things: much time away from the platform during lessons, many absences of a minute or more, and leaving in the same minutes as each other. The first two make a signature that can be computed for any class; the third tests whether high scorers form a group that leaves together. TE23's log has the same leave and return events as te24c's. TE22's has only return events, so its signature is an estimate.

    14Method notes

    • Same code, same rules. The te24c scripts were rerun on each class, with only inputs, paths and course dates changed. That includes the profile cut-offs, persistence outcomes, error spells and slopes, reading-time measures, lesson detection, the 11 group measures and the bootstrap settings.
    • Pooling the cohorts. TE22 pools three classes and TE23 two. Correlations use ranks within each class, so differences in level between classes do not count as an effect. Error slopes and lesson-page models pool students with student fixed effects. Cohort differences in level compare per-student values (Mann–Whitney); differences between correlations use Fisher's z.
    • Submitted vs started. For TE22 and TE23, an unsolved exercise counts as submitted when the log has an autograde event for it. In te22a's export a score of 0 often appears without any submission (331 of 428 exercise and checkpoint cells), so a 0 in an export means the exercise was started. te24c's 'gave up after a failed submission' therefore also includes exercises that were started but never submitted. The give-up rate and the profiles hardly change.
    • Timestamps. TE23's export times are one hour ahead of Swedish time and were shifted back; TE22's match the log.
    • Privacy. The TE22 code-zip file names include real names after the username. Only the username is kept.
    • Video-game signature. Away = blur → focus gaps of at least 2 s during detected lessons (TE22: previous event → focus). Co-absence = correlation between two students' minute-by-minute away indicators after removing the class's away rate in that minute. Links = pairs above the class's 90th percentile.
    Scripts and the findings summary are saved in the project under claude/te23/ (both cohorts). The te24c report remains the reference for the original analysis.
