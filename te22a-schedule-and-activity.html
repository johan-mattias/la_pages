When TE22A programmed, in lessons and out of them

The platform's event log records every page view, code run and edit with a timestamp. Filtered to the 27 TE22A students, it is enough to rebuild the lesson timetable for the whole year, see who was working in each lesson, and follow the work students did at school, in the evening and at weekends.

The timetable

Two lessons a week: Tuesday about 12:45–14:00 and Wednesday about 15:05–16:10. 59 lessons met your rule of two thirds of the class active at once; three more had only 14–17 students active. Five weeks had no lessons for any group: the autumn, Christmas, winter and Easter breaks.

Wednesdays are shorter

Measured from activity, the median Tuesday lesson lasts 74 minutes and the median Wednesday 61. If both are timetabled at 1 h 20, the Wednesday one loses about 20 minutes at the start or end.

In the lessons

Of 1,593 student-lessons, 67% were fully active, 18% partly (late start, early stop or a break over 20 minutes), 2% barely active and 13% had no activity at all. The spring was weaker than the autumn: 16 fully active students per lesson against 20.

Outside the lessons

35% of all active time happened outside lessons. After school and at weekends about 70% of it went to the assigned creative tasks and the final project; the scaffolded exercises were done mostly in lessons.

Deadlines and late nights

The week before the unit 7 task was due holds 17% of all out-of-lesson time, the last week of the final project 14%. 24 students worked between 23:00 and 05:00 at some point, eight of them for more than 100 minutes over the year.

Against the grades

Turning up tracks the grade modestly (ρ = 0.36; 0.55 with the final project grade). How fully active students were in the lessons they attended does not (ρ 0.05–0.07). After-school minutes correlate 0.64 with the final project grade.

The timetable, rebuilt from activity

For every minute of the year the log tells how many TE22A students were working on the platform (active within four minutes either side). A programming lesson is a stretch where at least 18 of the 27 students, two thirds, were active at the same time. Its start and end are where the count first rises above and finally falls below 9 students, a third of the class, on either side of that core. Short dips inside a lesson, such as a teacher introduction, do not split it.

Who was there, and how active

For each student and lesson, the share of the lesson's working minutes in which the student was active (an event within two minutes). A lesson counts as fully active when that share is at least 75% and the student never went more than 20 minutes without activity. Partly active covers late starts, early stops and long breaks; barely active is five minutes or less. A lesson with no events at all may mean the student was absent, or present but not using the platform.

Median activity in attended lessons was 91% of the working minutes, and the median longest pause 5 minutes. Among the 288 partly active lessons, 82 had a break over 20 minutes, 67 stopped more than 15 minutes early and 44 started more than 15 minutes late.

Lesson behaviour and work outside against the grades

Spearman's ρ with 95% bootstrap intervals, 27 students; the shaded band is roughly where p > 0.05. Being there matters more than how busy students were once there: attendance relates to both grades, while the share of each lesson spent active does not. Minutes after school relate strongly to the final project grade, which fits where the project work happened.

Work outside the lessons

Every active minute outside a lesson is placed in one of four bins: at school outside lessons (school days 08:00–17:00), after school (school days 17:00–08:00), weekends, and school breaks. Together they are 35% of all active time: 236 hours at school outside lessons, 136 after school, 108 at weekends and 20 during breaks.

Much of the at-school time comes straight after the Tuesday lesson. Activity stops at 14:00, but after a break of about ten minutes a median of eight students are back at work between 14:15 and 14:25, and at least nine on 13 of the 30 Tuesdays; over the year that hour holds 24 hours of work. Wednesday mornings and Friday afternoons are the other busy school-time slots.

Story thread · Improves

Work outside lessons is where the climbers of Chapter 1 stand out: they report working outside class as much as the strongest students. Across both cohorts, students who do much of their work in the evening and at weekends report the highest self-efficacy of four working styles (Chapter 3).

What they work on outside the lessons

Each active minute is tied to the page the student was on, and 95% of exercise and lesson-page minutes could be matched to a named curriculum item. In lessons, almost half the time goes to scaffolded exercises. After school and at weekends about 70% goes to the assigned creative tasks and the final project, and optional creative tasks take a larger share (10% at weekends) than in lessons (6%).

Exercises and lesson pages outside lessons: behind, level or ahead?

For curriculum items only, each minute compared with the unit the class was working on in lessons that week. Most out-of-lesson curriculum work is on the current unit; after school it leans towards catching up, at weekends towards working ahead.

Definitions

Active minute
A minute in which the student produced at least one event of any kind. Active hours are these minutes summed, so idle time with the page open does not count, unlike the platform's time-on-task figure.
Programming lesson
At least 18 of 27 students active within four minutes of the same moment. Stretches under two thirds that are less than 30 minutes apart belong to the same lesson.
Lesson start and end
Where the number of active students first rises above, and last falls below, 9 (a third of the class) around the two-thirds core.
Lesson with fewer active
At least a third but never two thirds of the class active together, at the usual lesson time. Not counted in the attendance figures.
Fully, partly, barely active
Fully: active in at least 75% of the lesson's working minutes, with no gap over 20 minutes. Barely: 5 active minutes or fewer. Partly: everything in between. Working minutes are those when at least a third of the class was active.
Late start, early stop
First activity more than 15 minutes after the lesson start; last activity more than 15 minutes before its end.
Contexts outside lessons
School hours: weekdays 08:00–17:00 in weeks with lessons, outside the lesson window plus five minutes either side. After school: the same days before 08:00 or after 17:00. Weekend: Saturday and Sunday. School break: weekdays in weeks when no group had lessons, public holidays and June.
Late night
23:00–05:00 outside lessons.
Behind, level, ahead
The unit of the exercise or lesson page compared with the median unit worked on in that week's lessons.
